# Packaging playbook (titles and thumbnails)

Built for FPI P1 at checkpoint 4 (2026-10-28) after Michael rejected descriptive titles. Use it for every episode.

## Rules
1. Legit bait only: the title teases the real core of the video and the video pays it off early. No deception, no fake stakes (Veritasium, "Clickbait is Unreasonably Effective").
2. Open a concrete gap, do not explain the answer. A title that states the mechanism ("it moves heat") closes the gap before the click. Point at a specific, self-relevant thing (Loewenstein information gap; Scott 2021 on relevance).
3. Familiar but unexpected: an everyday object plus one twist (Paddy Galloway).
4. No jargon in the title. Name the object, not the science term.
5. Title and thumbnail split the job: the thumbnail carries one question or image (max 4 words), the title adds the setup or the stakes and never repeats the thumbnail words.
6. 40-60 characters, hook in the first 40 (mobile truncation).
7. Strong patterns: a direct command/self-test ("Touch the side of..."), belief threat ("You've been wrong about..."), paradox with a concrete object ("X doesn't exist. Your Y proves it.").
8. Titles can be changed after upload; a retitle can produce a second view spike. Revisit if CTR is weak in the first week.

## P1 result
- Title: Touch the Side of Your Fridge Right Now (39 chars)
- Thumbnail: thumb-1, "WHY IS IT HOT?" on the cutaway with warm sides.
- Pairing: the title is the action, the thumbnail is the result; the video opens on that exact moment.
- Rejected (too descriptive or closed the gap): "Why Your Fridge Has to Get Hot to Keep Food Cold", "Your Fridge Has a Heater Inside. Here's Why.", "The Hidden Heat That Keeps Your Food Cold", "Your Fridge Is Secretly a Heater", "Your Fridge Doesn't Make Cold. It Pumps Heat Into Your Kitchen."

## When packaging happens (Michael 2026-11-02)
Title and thumbnails are made after the alpha cut of the video exists and before the final YouTube upload, so they are built from the real frames and the real hook. The title can be drafted earlier (topic stage) but is locked together with the thumbnail at that point.
Thumbnails are built on the strongest real-looking frame (3D hero), never the flat diagram: one idea, max 3 words, readable at 160 px, never repeating the title. Make two that differ in idea, not styling, and run them in YouTube Studio's Test & Compare.
