# P5: The four-stroke engine (working title "Your Car Engine Only Pushes One Stroke in Four")

Picked by Michael 2026-11-25 18:10 ET from the topic catalogue (car engine, four-stroke, overall 5.00, the top score). Target: Saturday Dec 5, 9 AM ET (P4 moved up to Nov 28).

## The competition (from the catalogue scan; packaging agent refreshes this)
- Saturated mechanism space: Animagraffs, Jared Owen, Engineering Explained and many others have clean 3D walk-throughs of the four strokes.
- Gap we fill: the *design bargain*. The engine is mostly idle, coasting and pumping. One stroke in four does the work, the other three are paid for by stored momentum, and most of the fuel still leaves as heat. Nobody frames the engine as a machine built around its own inefficiency.
- Keep clear of P1: heat is one beat (the bill), not the thesis.

## Format: one cycle in slow motion (new; P1 paradox, P2 detective, P3 engineering challenge, P4 heist)
The whole video follows a single cycle of one cylinder at idle, about a tenth of a second of real time, stretched across nine minutes. An on-screen clock shows real elapsed time (milliseconds) and the crank angle. Each chapter is one stroke; history and design asides hang off the stroke they explain.

## Beats (draft; the research decides the final wording and numbers)
1. **Cold open, on the viewer.** At a red light your engine turns about 13 times a second (idle), and in each cylinder only one stroke in four pushes. The other three, the engine is being carried. Freeze. Clock starts at 0.000 s. Intro sting.
2. **Intake (0 to 180 degrees).** The piston falls and the engine acts as an air pump; the throttle at idle makes it work against a partial vacuum (pumping loss, if sourced). Valves open before and close after the stroke (timing, conceptual).
3. **Compression (180 to 360).** Squeezing the mix, ratio about 8 to 12:1 for petrol (allowed range from the fact check). Why not squeeze more: knock, and what octane really measures (resistance to knock, not energy).
4. **The spark comes early.** Ignition before top dead centre because burning takes time; the flame front, not an explosion (if sourced).
5. **Power (360 to 540).** The only push. The flywheel and the other cylinders store and share it (firing order, why more cylinders run smoother).
6. **Exhaust (540 to 720).** Pushing out the burnt gas, still hot: the bill. Of the fuel's energy only a fraction reaches the wheels (fueleconomy.gov figure, fact-checked), the rest is heat out the exhaust and the radiator.
7. **Where the cycle came from.** Beau de Rochas's 1862 description, Nicolaus Otto's 1876 engine and patent (who they were); Otto's patent later overturned in Germany because of the earlier description (if two sources support it).
8. **Close.** Clock reads about 0.15 s: one cycle. The engine is a machine that coasts three quarters of the time and runs on its own momentum. "Designed otherwise."
