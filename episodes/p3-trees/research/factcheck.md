## Do not say

- **1.1**: Do not give the discoverers' names (Chris Atkins, Michael Taylor) as checked fact: Guinness only.
- **1.1**: Do not say Hyperion will always be the tallest: NPS says the title 'will change again in the near future'.
- **1.1**: Do not imply viewers should visit it: NPS says it is off trail and visitors have damaged its base.
- **1.2**: Do not state 'the tallest flowering plant in the world' as settled: only Treelogic (reproducing a Forestry Tasmania release) says it, Guinness lists a yellow meranti at 98.53 m only 1.3 m shorter, and rankings change with each remeasurement (checker's own knowledge, unsourced: a Sabah meranti called Menara was reported at about 100.8 m in 2019).
- **1.2**: Do not give a precise height (99.82 vs 100-101 m conflict).
- **1.2**: Guinness does not print the name 'Centurion'; the link between the Guinness 99.82 m entry and Centurion is by location only. Use the name only alongside 'about 100 m'.
- **1.3**: Do not call the Doerner Fir the tallest Douglas-fir now: after the fire JPR says it is no longer even the tallest tree in Oregon.
- **1.3**: Do not say exactly 99.7 m or 327 ft as the only figure; sources differ by 2 ft.
- **1.3**: Do not claim a specific cause of the fire (JPR: still under investigation, lightning ruled out).
- **1.4**: Do not say 'the tallest giant sequoias reach about 90 m' as a general fact: only one opened source (Ambrose, a 15-tree study range). If used, say 'some giant sequoias top 90 metres'.
- **1.4**: Do not reuse PBS's comparison ('almost the height of an 11-story building'): it is wrong for 272 ft (NPS uses 26 storeys for the largest sequoias).
- **1.4**: Do not use the NPS General Grant metric figure (88.7 m for 268.1 ft) - internal NPS conversion error per claims file.
- **1.5**: Do not give an exact storey count (e.g. 'a 38-storey building'): no source compares Hyperion to storeys; it is arithmetic and depends on storey height.
- **1.5**: Do not borrow Nature News' '35-storey skyscraper': that is for the 130 m theoretical limit, not Hyperion.
- **1.6**: Do not say 'the tree uses no energy at all' or 'water transport costs the tree nothing': living root cells spend energy loading ions (root pressure) and guard cells actively open and close stomata.
- **1.6**: Do not quote the Bentrup/Tyree 'can't have a heart' line as support: Bentrup is a critic of cohesion-tension; the line is Tyree's subtitle quoted inside a challenger's review.
- **1.7**: Do not say transpiration stops completely at night in all trees ('stops' is textbook shorthand; some species keep a little night-time water loss - checker's general knowledge).
- **1.7**: If using 'the tree outside your window is doing it right now', qualify it: daytime, growing season, tree in leaf.
- **2.1**: Do not say exactly 10.3 m is the practical pump limit: in a real pump water boils at low pressure, so practical limits are a bit lower, and the limit falls at altitude (checker/researcher physics knowledge, unsourced).
- **2.1**: Do not quote Brown's 10.4 m alongside 10.3 m; pick 'about 10 metres'.
- **2.2**: Do not say the vacuum 'sucks' or 'pulls' the water - the point of the beat is that it doesn't.
- **2.3**: Do not give the historical limit as 10.3 m; the period figure is '18 braccia, about 11 m' (a braccio varied).
- **2.3**: Do not say 'Florentine pump makers' or 'well diggers': no opened source says so. Chemistry World: 'rivers and wells'.
- **2.3**: Do not state one specific wrong explanation for Galileo: Museo Galileo says he blamed the limited 'force of the vacuum', Chemistry World says 'the weight of water'. Say he 'couldn't explain it correctly'.
- **2.3**: The 1630 Baliani letter was about a siphon over a ~21 m hill, not a pump; if used, describe it that way (Museo Galileo only).
- **2.3**: Do not credit Torricelli alone with the first tube experiments without noting Gasparo Berti's water-tube experiments around 1640 (Britannica and Chemistry World both mention Berti).
- **2.4**: Do not say 'exactly 11 times'.
- **3.1**: Do not present the ion-loading pathway in detail as settled; sources describe it differently (Missouri: solutes 'in cells inside the endodermis'; Ewers: linked to nutrient uptake, especially nitrate).
- **3.1**: Do not say root pressure happens in all plants (UMN: 'only happens in some plants, but not in them all').
- **3.2**: Do not show guttation on a giant redwood; the sources' examples are grasses (fescue, ribbon grass) and small plants.
- **3.2**: Do not call the droplets dew.
- **3.3**: Do not list butternut as a root-pressure example: North Country Now (Cornell CCE) says its roots push sap, but CSBSJU's maple-sap page groups butternut with sugar maple as a stem-pressure exuder, and Cirelli et al. 2008 study butternut alongside maple for stem pressure. Conflict.
- **3.3**: Grapevine is single-source (CSBSJU); birch has two (CSBSJU, North Country Now).
- **3.3**: Walnut root pressure data are for Juglans regia only and only in autumn and spring; winter pressures in the same trees were of stem origin.
- **3.4**: Do not explain the exact maple mechanism as settled: Cirelli et al. 2008 describe two competing theories (physical Milburn-O'Malley vs osmotic), and North Country Now ends 'the mystery of sap flow continues'.
- **3.4**: Do not use the UMN open textbook for maple: it wrongly says root pressure drives sugar maple sap in spring.
- **3.4**: Do not say maple has no root pressure ever under any conditions; say maple sap runs are not driven by it.
- **3.5**: Do not give one precise root pressure value as universal; values vary by species and season.
- **3.5**: Do not say root pressure could lift water 30 m in a real tree: the metre figures ignore friction.
- **3.6**: Do not say 'conifers have little or no root pressure': O'Leary & Kramer 1964 saw no exudation from detopped loblolly pine and white spruce seedlings, but the USDA Forest Service (1981) collected root-pressure exudate from detopped Douglas-fir, grand fir and noble fir seedlings. Both used seedlings, not tall trees.
- **3.6**: Do not name a conifer species as lacking root pressure; it is single-source and seedling-only.
- **3.7**: Do not quote CSBSJU's 'at least 3 MPa required for a 100 m tree' as a measurement: it is a teaching estimate (0.02 MPa/m friction from a Poiseuille calculation plus 0.01 MPa/m gravity).
- **4.1**: The name 'Jurin's law' is not in any opened source (it is standard physics); fine for an on-screen label but not checked here.
- **4.2**: Do not say tree vessels are 'up to 500 um' as if typical of trees: the >500 um figure is for tropical lianas (vines).
- **4.2**: Do not give 'vessels from under 20 um': no opened source states a lower vessel bound.
- **4.2**: Do not treat OpenStax's 'radii as small as 2.5 x 10^-5 m' (50 um wide) as the narrowest xylem; it is a textbook example value and conflicts with 10 um tracheids.
- **4.3**: Do not say capillarity lifts water 'under 1-2 m' in the narrowest tracheids: about 3 m is correct for 10 um-wide tracheids.
- **4.3**: Do not say 'about 180 times narrower than xylem' as a general fact: 180x is OpenStax's comparison with one 25 um radius; versus a 5 um radius tracheid it is only ~34x. Use 'far narrower'.
- **4.3**: Do not imply capillarity could keep water flowing even if pipes were narrow enough (CSBSJU: it 'would only move to the top of the plant once').
- **4.4**: Do not say water actually rises 3 km in a tree; that is a theoretical capillary height.
- **4.4**: Do not give a precise pore size; say 'a few nanometres'.
- **4.4**: Do not conflate these cell-wall pores in the leaf with pit membrane pores (beat 6).
- **5.1**: Do not say Dixon and Joly were the first or only originators: Bentrup credits Boehm (1893) jointly, and Brown says Askenasy independently published a detailed account (1895-96) and that 'arguably equal honours' go to Boehm.
- **5.1**: Do not say 'first proposed in 1895' (TCD) without 1894: Britannica, Brown and Bentrup all give 1894 for the first publication.
- **5.2**: Do not give a single date for Strasburger's experiments: Brown says evidence 'by 1891', TCD says Dixon and Joly saw them in 1892, Dixon and Joly's own 1895 paper says Bonn, summer 1893, and the LJWorld article says 1893.
- **5.2**: 'The flow stopped only when the leaves were killed' rests on one newspaper source (LJWorld/Baltimore Sun); say it only as 'according to accounts of the experiment' or leave it out.
- **5.2**: The 70-foot oak and picric acid details: oak and 70 ft are LJWorld only; picric acid has two sources (LJWorld, CSBSJU).
- **5.3**: Do not show the stomata as the evaporating surface; evaporation is from wet cell walls inside the leaf, stomata are the exit.
- **5.4**: Do not quote OpenStax Physics' 'The question has not been completely resolved' as current; cohesion-tension is the mainstream explanation (see 5.11).
- **5.5**: Do not say xylem pressure is always below absolute zero; Wheeler & Stroock say 'often'.
- **5.6**: Do not quote a single 'typical' xylem tension for tall trees as measured; no opened source gives tall-tree values (Koch's values were abstract-only).
- **5.6**: Do not use the synthetic-tree '-1.0 MPa or lower' (Wheeler & Stroock) as a tree measurement; it is a hydrogel device.
- **5.7**: Do not give one value for 'the' tensile strength of water: methods disagree by about five-fold and the reason is unresolved (Herbert: 'Possible reasons for this discrepancy are considered').
- **5.7**: Do not say trees use water's full strength: xylem fails far earlier, at a few MPa, by air seeding at pits.
- **5.8**: Dixon's 1922 glass pressure chamber that 'exploded' is single-source (TCD). If used, attribute it ('according to Trinity College Dublin's history').
- **5.8**: Do not say the pressure chamber is beyond dispute: Bentrup 2017 (Zimmermann school) contests how its readings should be interpreted.
- **5.8**: The first name 'Per' is not in an opened source for this claim (CSBSJU mentions 'Per Scholander stories'); acceptable but light support.
- **5.9**: Do not say the trunk shrinks 'a few tenths of a millimetre' as a radius: WSL's 200 um is a radius change; most of the whole-stem change is the living bark, not the wood.
- **5.9**: Do not attribute all shrinkage to tension in the wood; Oberhuber shows inner bark changes ~5x more than xylem.
- **5.9**: These values are Scots pine and Swiss trees, not redwoods.
- **5.9**: Sevanto's top-first lag applies to xylem diameter; for whole-stem diameter the treetop lagged. Only use the 'pull travels down' visual for the wood.
- **5.10**: Do not say 'powered solely by the sun' without the dry air: OpenStax frames the driver as the energy difference between soil water and atmospheric water; transpiration continues on dry, cloudy days.
- **5.10**: Do not say the tree spends no energy on water at all (root ion uptake and stomatal control cost energy).
- **5.11**: Do not say the centrifuge validation came 'later' than the challenge; the 1995 test was contemporary with it (checker's knowledge that the probe challenge began in the early 1990s is unsourced; avoid sequencing).
- **5.11**: Do not present the multi-force theory as mainstream or as refuted by name; present cohesion-tension as accepted, not as beyond all debate.
- **5.11**: Do not name 'Ulrich' unless needed: the first name appears only in Bentrup's citation 'U. Zimmermann'.
- **6.1**: Do not imply drought is the only cause: freezing also causes embolism in some species.
- **6.2**: Do not claim how the very first bubble in a healthy pipe forms is settled (Kaack et al.: 'embolism spreading remains puzzling').
- **6.2**: Say 'usually spreads', not 'always'.
- **6.3**: Do not say 'you can hear a tree drinking/cavitating' or present a sound effect as a real recording: modern detection is ultrasonic; any SFX must be labelled an illustration (slowed or shifted).
- **6.3**: Do not call the 1966 clicks ultrasonic or audible: the opened summary says neither.

- **6.3**: Citation detail: Springer's title is 'The conduction of sap. II.'; PROMETHEUS lists it as 'I.' and spells the author 'Millburn'. Use Milburn & Johnson 1966, Planta 69:43-52.

- **6.5**: Do not quote species P50 values: none were gathered or checked.

- **6.6**: Do not present the 95% figure as a general rule: four conifer species, leaves, lab-type setting.

- **6.7**: Do not say embolism is always permanent: Stewart's 'no refilling' result is in sunflowers, not trees.
