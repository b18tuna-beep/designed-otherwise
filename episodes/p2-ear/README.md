# P2: the inner ear (working title "Your Ears Make Sounds")

Locked by Michael 2026-11-12 (catalogue topic "human ear (cochlea)", overall 4.88).
Twist: the inner ear is not a passive microphone. Outer hair cells physically lengthen and shorten to amplify faint sound,
and this amplifier is strong enough that healthy ears give off faint sounds of their own (otoacoustic emissions);
newborn hearing screening listens for exactly that.

## Working beat outline (to be confirmed by research; nothing here is a claim yet)
1. Cold open: the newborn hearing test listens for sound coming OUT of the ear.
2. The microphone model: outer ear, canal, eardrum, the three ossicles; why air-to-fluid needs the bones (impedance matching).
3. The cochlea: fluid-filled spiral; basilar membrane stiff and narrow at the base (high notes), wide and floppy at the apex (low notes); the travelling wave.
4. Two kinds of hair cells: inner hair cells sense, outer hair cells amplify (prestin, electromotility, the cochlear amplifier).
5. The ear talks back: otoacoustic emissions (Kemp 1978), spontaneous emissions, why screening uses them.
6. The cost of an amplifier: outer hair cells are the first casualties of noise and do not regrow in mammals; why high pitches go first.
