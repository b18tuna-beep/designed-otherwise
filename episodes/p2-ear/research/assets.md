## Notes

**Footage verdict.** No real footage of an outer hair cell changing length is public domain or CC0. All the well-known clips (Ashmore at UCL, Brownell, Santos-Sacchi at Yale, Gummer at Tuebingen) are university-owned. Beat 4 has to be our own chalk animation. The Tuebingen page is still a good motion reference: outer and inner hair cells move in opposite phase, and the data were taken in the third turn at 936 Hz. The NIDCD videos (V1, V2) are probably public domain, but the producer is never named and the only copy is a YouTube embed, so using them would mean a YouTube rip. Skip them unless an official master turns up.

**Cold open.** The four Eglin AFB photos (P1-P4, Feb 2011, one session) are the backbone: public domain, and the caption of P1 names the device as an OAE screener (Eroscan). Rules: credit "U.S. Air Force photo / Samuel King Jr."; never show or say the baby's name (it is in the captions); don't imply DoD endorsement.

**Gray's plates.** Extract from the archive.org 1918 scan (G1). Picks: fig. 919 (chain of ossicles) for beat 2; 923 (cochlea and vestibule) and 928 (cross-section of cochlea) for beat 3; 931 (spiral organ of Corti) and 932 (lamina reticularis, with rows of outer hair cells) for beat 4. Chalk-trace them or tint them to the slate palette per the style bible.

**The ear's own sound (A5).** No public domain or CC0 recording of a real OAE was found. Synthesize it and label it on screen as "simulated, amplified":
- SOAE: one to three steady pure tones between 1 and 5 kHz (young-adult mean about 2.4 kHz; newborn mean about 3 to 4.4 kHz). Real level is -10 to 30 dB SPL (adult mean about 4, newborn about 9 to 11), which is near or below the threshold of hearing, so playback needs a huge gain. Say so in narration.
- Probe test (TEOAE): a click at about 80 to 84 dB SPL peak equivalent. Build it as a rectangular pulse of about 80 to 100 microseconds, or band-limit A1. The echo is 15 to 30+ dB SPL in healthy infants, about 50 to 70 dB quieter than the click (my arithmetic from Kemp's numbers). It returns between about 3.5 and 12 ms after the click, high frequencies first (latency 2 to 23 ms), with most energy at 1 to 4 kHz. A synthesized downward chirp with a decaying envelope is an honest picture of this.
- DPOAE (if shown): two tones, f2/f1 = 1.2, L1 = L2 + 10 dB, about 55 to 65 dB SPL; the ear answers at 2f1 - f2.
- These figures go into the claims dossier via the normal fact check before any of them is spoken.

Hero modelling tip: make the basilar membrane a ribbon whose width ramps about 4-5x from base to apex inside a duct that narrows. That reproduces the counterintuitive point (the bony tube gets narrower toward the apex while the membrane gets wider) honestly.
