## Beats (draft; the research decides the final wording and numbers)
1. **Cold open, on the viewer.** The key in your pocket: your lock has never looked at its shape. It checks one thing, whether a row of tiny gaps lines up. (1 s beat after the first line.) And in many buildings, it is built to accept a second key you have never seen. Intro sting.
2. **Inside the lock.** Plug, housing, pin stacks (key pin, driver pin, spring), the shear line. No key: the drivers straddle the line, the plug can't turn. Right key: every split lands on the line. The pins only feel the height at each cut, not the key's outline (the keyway profile is a separate, cruder check).
3. **The second key.** Master keying: an extra pin in a stack gives two heights that work, so the landlord's key and yours both open your door. (Blaze 2003 on master-keyed systems: conceptual only.)
4. **A very old idea.** Pin locks in ancient Egypt / the Near East: wooden pins dropping into a bolt, a key that lifts them. Linus Yale Sr. and Jr. (who they were) shrink it into a cylinder and a small flat key (1840s-1860s patents; US patents are public domain).
5. **1851, the unpickable lock.** The Bramah challenge in a London shop window since about 1790; Alfred C. Hobbs (who he was), at the Great Exhibition, opens it and a Chubb detector lock. The scandal: no mechanical lock is unpickable; the only question is how long.
6. **Why pins can be beaten at all: tolerances.** Nothing is machined perfectly: the holes aren't in a perfect line, so under turning force one pin binds before the others. Conceptual, as a design flaw, NOT a tutorial: no tools, no technique steps, no amounts.
7. **The answers.** Security pins (spools), tighter tolerances, sidebars, standards grades (ANSI/BHMA). And the twist: most burglars don't pick, they force the door (if the sources support it). The lock buys time; it's a compromise between cost, convenience and seconds.
8. **Close.** Your lock isn't a reader. It's a gate that checks five heights, a 4,000-year-old trick, and a bargain. "Designed otherwise."

## Safety line
Educational design story. No step-by-step picking or bypass instructions, no tool lists, no "how to get into a door". Same level as an encyclopedia article.
