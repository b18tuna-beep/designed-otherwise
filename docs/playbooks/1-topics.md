# 1. Topics and lineup

Goal: something ordinary (an object, a system, a body part, a natural structure; not only machines, Michael 2026-11-02) whose real design surprises people, with enough checkable sources to back every line.

## Steps
1. Candidates: start from the scored catalogue (studio `research/catalogue/`: 160 topics, research supply vs YouTube demand). One twist each: the thing works the opposite way to how it looks.
2. Demand: check YouTube search and outlier videos for the object (run research in Actions; the workspace reaches GitHub, npm and PyPI only).
3. Sourceability: can every core claim get two independent sources (manufacturer service manuals, ENERGY STAR, DOE, university and engineering references)? If not, drop the topic.
4. Packaging test before work starts: write a title and thumbnail pair under `style/packaging.md`. If the gap can't be opened without lying, pick another topic.
5. Production read per candidate: what is 3D (Blender hero), what is 2D (Motion Canvas), what public-domain footage exists (US government incl. NIH/NASA/NARA, LoC, pre-1931 works, expired patents). Verify each asset's license before use.
6. Propose 2-3 candidates to Michael; lock a topic only on his word.

## Rules
- Format variety between episodes (inauthentic-content policy): not every video is the same loop of beats.
- No topic whose core claim is a single-source number.
